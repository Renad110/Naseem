    Why Naseem

    Mass gatherings in extreme heat

    During the thermal season, gatherings like the Hajj pilgrimage carry severe health risks. Conventional prevention leans on awareness campaigns, cooling stations and manual emergency response.

    🥵

    Heat exhaustion

    Long hours outdoors under intense sun, often in crowded areas with limited shade.

    💧

    Severe dehydration

    Fluid and electrolyte loss through sweat is hard to notice until symptoms appear.

    🚑

    Life-threatening heatstroke

    Delays between detection and treatment — and imprecise location — cost critical minutes.

    ⚠️

    The gap: commercial wearables are passive monitors — they alert, but deliver no therapy at the point of distress. Standard dispatch systems often lack real-time telemetry and precise geospatial tracking.

    🔬
    1 · Continuous sensing

    Microfluidic channels sample sweat; pH, Na⁺, K⁺ and skin temperature are read non-invasively.

    🧠
    2 · Risk detection

    The microcontroller compares readings with critical thresholds; the indicator turns amber and the app reminds the pilgrim to drink water and seek shade.

    ❄️
    3 · Autonomous cooling

    At the skin-temperature threshold, the PCM hydrogel triggers a localized endothermic reaction, while moisturizers and a mild analgesic are released.

    📡
    4 · Emergency dispatch

    An SOS with GPS coordinates and live vitals goes to the medical command center for rapid triage and response.

    IoT ecosystem

    Pilgrim app + emergency dispatch

    The patch pairs by NFC and streams over BLE. The app adds GPS and routes emergencies to a medical command center. Press SOS to try it.
